Most happy customers will leave a Google review. They just don't think to do it on their own. If your competitors have 150 reviews and you have 12, it's rarely because their work is better. It's because they ask, every time, in a way that makes it easy to say yes.

Here's how to do the same, without awkward conversations or chasing people for weeks.

Why Google reviews matter so much for local businesses

When someone searches for a plumber, cleaner or electrician near them, Google shows a small map with three businesses. Google says it ranks those results on relevance, distance and prominence, and that your review count and rating are part of prominence. So reviews do two jobs at once: they help you show up, and they convince the person reading to call you rather than the next business.

1. Ask at the right moment

The best time to ask is straight after the job, while the customer is still pleased with the result. A day later is fine. A month later, most people have moved on. If your work finishes on site, a text that arrives within a few hours of leaving tends to get far more responses than one sent at the end of the week.

2. Make it one tap

Every extra step loses people. Don't tell customers to "search for us on Google". Send them your direct review link, so one tap opens the review box with your business already selected. You'll find the link in your Google Business Profile under "Ask for reviews".

3. Keep the message short and personal

Use the customer's first name, mention the job and keep it to two or three lines. For example:

Hi Sarah, thanks for having us out to fix the hot water system today. If you have 30 seconds, a quick Google review would really help our small team: [link]

A personal touch, like a photo of your team or a thank-you image with the customer's name on it, makes the message stand out from the usual marketing texts.

4. Follow up, politely

Plenty of people mean to leave a review and simply forget. One or two friendly reminders over the following week can double your response rate. Stop the reminders as soon as they've left a review, and never send more than a couple.

5. Don't forget your past customers

If you've been in business for a few years, you probably have hundreds of happy past customers who were never asked. A "review reactivation" campaign sends them a request too. Spread these messages out over a few weeks rather than sending them all at once, so your reviews arrive steadily instead of in one suspicious spike.

6. Reply to every review

Replying shows future customers that someone is listening, and Google itself recommends it. Thank people for good reviews, and answer negative ones calmly and professionally. A thoughtful reply to a bad review often does more for your reputation than another five-star review.

What not to do

  • Don't offer incentives. Discounts, gift cards or prize draws in exchange for reviews break Google's policies.
  • Don't only ask happy customers. Filtering out unhappy customers before they reach Google, called review gating, is also against Google's rules. Ask everyone the same way.
  • Don't write reviews for customers or post them from staff, friends or family.
  • Follow the Spam Act. Identify your business in every text or email and include a simple way to opt out.
